TO THE FELLOWSHIP

Python

Python was the second programming language I ever picked up, introduced originally back during my school years. Right from the start, it became my most relied-upon tool in the belt. What makes it special is how effortlessly it bends to your will. Clean syntax, fast iteration, and the ability to turn an idea into working logic almost instantly is genuinely a great experience. It has always been a language I genuinely enjoy using.

However, that exact fluidity is what first raised eyebrows with the local overseers. I remember a school assignment where we were handed a basic prompt- something straightforward and rigidly scoped. Instead of writing the standard, expected script, I, along with my fellow code-weavers, ended up treating it like a full-scale systems project. We built a massively over-engineered solution complete with external packages and unnecessary architectural layers just to see how far we could push it.

Looking back, that was probably the exact moment the ministerium started getting suspicious of my code-weaving habits. I guess when you start turning standard assignment into an over-engineered arcane ritual, people tend to notice.
